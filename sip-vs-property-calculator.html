What this calculator deliberately does not price

Stability and flexibility. Consider how each option fits your work, family and likely moves. This calculator does not assign a rupee value to those preferences or assess tenancy and ownership rights.

Following the plan. Both paths assume that every monthly saving is invested. If you spend the saving, skip contributions or face an unplanned cost, your outcome will differ from the model.

Access to your money. Consider how quickly you may need cash and the concentration of your assets. This model does not estimate sale times, exit costs or investment redemption restrictions.

Tax and eligibility. This comparison excludes personal tax effects. Have the rules applicable to your income, property and chosen investments checked separately before making a decision.

How this model works and where it stops. It amortises the loan monthly, grows rent at your stated rate, compounds the invested surplus monthly, and grows the property value at your stated appreciation rate. Both paths use the same monthly housing budget: whichever costs less invests that month's saving at the stated investment return. Buying net worth is property value minus outstanding debt plus the buyer's investment portfolio. Renting net worth is the down payment and transaction costs invested from day one plus the renter's monthly savings. Contributions are added at each month end. This includes buyer savings when rent exceeds ownership costs, including after the loan is repaid. It excludes income tax deductions on home loans, capital gains tax on either path, brokerage, GST on under-construction property, prepayment, and rental yield if you let the flat out. Investment returns are assumptions, not forecasts — equity returns are volatile and past performance does not predict future results. This is general information for comparison, not investment advice; consult a SEBI-registered adviser before acting.
