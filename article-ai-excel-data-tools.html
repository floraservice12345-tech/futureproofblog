Every spreadsheet tool now claims to have AI in it. Very few of them change what you can actually do with a workbook on a Tuesday afternoon, and the ones that do are not always the ones with the biggest marketing budget.

This is a working comparison of the tools that matter for spreadsheet analysis in 2026, what each one is genuinely good at, what it costs an Indian buyer, and — the part most listicles skip — the limitation that will bite you three weeks in.

If you only read one line

If you already pay for Microsoft 365, add Copilot. If you already pay for ChatGPT or Claude, use their spreadsheet integrations before buying anything new. If you are building recurring dashboards rather than answering one-off questions, the answer is Power Pivot and Power Query — which you already own and which no AI tool replaces.

What Each One Is Actually For

Microsoft Copilot in Excel

The most natural fit if your organisation is already on Microsoft 365, because it lives inside the application rather than beside it. It reads the sheet you have open, writes formulas in place, explains what a nested formula is doing and builds pivot tables from a plain-English instruction.

The limitation: it does not do true bulk row-by-row processing — asking it to classify 8,000 rows one at a time is not what it is built for — and it is Excel only. If your data lives in Google Sheets, this is not your tool.

Claude for Excel

Noticeably stronger than most alternatives on workbooks with many interlinked tabs, which is exactly the shape of most real financial models. It follows references across sheets and explains model logic rather than just describing the visible cells.

The limitation: large datasets hit context limits, and like most of this category it will not batch-process tens of thousands of rows.

ChatGPT for Excel & Sheets

The pragmatic default for individuals, because most people who would buy a spreadsheet AI tool are already paying for ChatGPT. Good for ad-hoc questions, formula authoring and quick chart generation. Free and entry-tier plans are meaningfully limited.

The limitation: big workbooks exceed the context window, and results degrade quietly rather than failing loudly — which is worse.

GPT for Work

The one genuinely differentiated pick on this list. It handles bulk row-by-row operations that the chat-based tools will not: classify 5,000 customer comments, extract a city name from 20,000 messy address strings, translate a product catalogue. If that is your problem, this solves it and the others do not.

The limitation: it is a processing tool, not a BI or dashboard platform. Do not expect visual output.

Numerous.ai

The cheapest useful entry on the list. It adds AI as a formula you drag down a column, which is an unreasonably good fit for repetitive text work — sentiment tags, categorisation, short summaries per row.

The limitation: no charts, no pivot tables, no agentic behaviour. It does one thing.

Julius AI

Upload a file, ask questions in plain English, get charts and statistical output without writing code. Useful for people who need analysis but do not have an analyst. The free tier is capped tightly enough to be a demo rather than a plan.

Power BI

Not a spreadsheet tool and not really an AI tool, but it belongs here because it is what people actually need when they say "I want an AI dashboard". If the requirement is a report that refreshes on a schedule and is shared with a team, Power BI is the answer and a chat interface is not.

The limitation: a real learning curve, and Copilot features sit behind a higher tier.

The Thing No Tool on This List Replaces

Power Query and Power Pivot

If your monthly reporting job is "take four exports, clean them, join them, and produce the same summary" — that is not an AI problem. It is a Power Query problem, and Power Query is already inside the Excel you own. Build the transformation once, and every subsequent month is a refresh button.

Power Pivot extends this to data models spanning millions of rows with relationships and DAX measures — far past what a worksheet handles, at no additional licence cost on most desktop Excel editions.

The most common expensive mistake we see is a team paying for an AI add-on to re-do by conversation what a twenty-minute Power Query build would have automated permanently.

How to Choose in Three Questions

  1. Is this a one-off question or a recurring report? One-off → a chat-based tool you already pay for. Recurring → Power Query, then Power BI if it needs publishing.
  2. Do you need per-row processing at volume? If yes, GPT for Work or Numerous.ai. The chat tools will disappoint you here regardless of price.
  3. Where does the data live? Excel and Microsoft 365 → Copilot. Google Sheets → Gemini. Files on your desktop and no subscription → Julius or ChatGPT.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a genuinely free option?

Yes, several — but with real caps. Gemini is included with Google Workspace plans, ChatGPT's free tier handles light spreadsheet questions, and Ajelix and Julius both have free tiers limited to a small number of requests per month. For anything sustained, expect to pay.

Can I upload company financial data to these tools?

Check your organisation's policy first, and check the vendor's data retention terms. Business and enterprise tiers generally offer stronger guarantees than consumer plans about training on your data. Entry tiers on some platforms delete uploaded files automatically, which is a privacy feature and a workflow problem at the same time.

Will AI replace Excel skills?

It has not so far, and the pattern is the opposite: these tools are most valuable to people who already know what a correct answer looks like. Someone who understands lookup logic and pivot structure gets far more out of Copilot than someone hoping it will substitute for that understanding.

The Bottom Line

Start with what you already pay for. Add a bulk-processing tool only if you have a genuine bulk-processing problem. Learn Power Query before you buy anything, because it will eliminate more repetitive work than any subscription on this page.

The teams getting real value from AI in spreadsheets in 2026 are not the ones with the most tools. They are the ones who worked out which of their reporting was a one-off question and which was a process — and only bought software for the first category.
