A local shop or maker may already have products people like but few ways to show them beyond the neighbourhood. ONDC can be one route to online discovery. Before paying for onboarding or preparing a large catalogue, understand who does what and what costs apply to your business.

ONDC is an open network, not one store or a guarantee of orders. Sellers generally connect through a Seller Network Participant using a seller application. ONDC says these participants help digitise catalogues, disperse payments and train sellers in e-commerce practices. Each participant may offer different services and terms.

Pick products you can describe and fulfil consistently

A small pilot makes mistakes cheaper to find. Choose a few products with stable availability, straightforward packing and a delivery area you understand. A fragile, highly customised or short-life item may require a more demanding process than an ordinary stocked product.

Prepare a small catalogue

Start with products you can keep in stock and fulfil reliably. For each item, prepare an accurate name, size or quantity, price, clear photographs, dispatch time and current stock. Include packaging needs and delivery restrictions. If an item is seasonal or made to order, say so plainly.

A listing should answer a buyer’s basic questions. Do not show an item as available if you cannot dispatch it. Online cancellations can cost time and customer trust.

Compare participants before joining

Check ONDC’s Seller Network Participants directory. Ask providers that support your product category:

  • Is there an onboarding fee or recurring charge?
  • What commission, payment or logistics costs may apply?
  • Who can edit, pause or remove the catalogue?
  • How are orders, returns, complaints and cancellations handled?
  • When are payments settled, and what deductions appear?
  • Which buyer applications can currently discover the catalogue?
  • What support is available if stock or delivery details are wrong?

There is no universal fee or sales result. Read the provider’s current terms and estimate the net amount you would keep. Be cautious of anyone promising guaranteed orders or income.

The ONDC participant directory states that listing does not constitute endorsement. Compare the actual contract, category coverage and support arrangements. Keep a copy of the terms and the date you received them; do not rely only on a sales call.

Test the order journey

Begin with a few products and a delivery area you can serve. Confirm that order notifications reach the person responsible for packing. Check the listing on a buyer application where possible, and verify the displayed price and stock.

For each order, record the amount paid, settlement amount, product cost, packaging, delivery, time and any refund or return.

Contribution per order = net settled proceeds − product cost − packaging − delivery and other order costs not already deducted from settlement

Reconcile the settlement statement and count every charge once. If a fee or delivery charge was withheld before settlement, do not subtract it again. Separately record the time you spend on each order so you can judge the return for your work.

If contribution is too low, review the product bundle, packaging, delivery area or price. Do not discount before checking which costs can realistically change.

Use ONDC’s current resources index and network policies as starting points, then confirm the order, payment and complaint processes with your chosen participant.

Starting from ₹415, subtracting the ₹45 fee and ₹40 delivery again would understate the remainder by ₹85. Match the statement to the order ID and the actual bank credit. In real orders, taxes, refunds, subsidies and other adjustments may appear separately; understand those lines before deciding what is available to keep.

Measure the time spent listing, answering questions, packing and resolving issues separately. The ₹195 in this example is not a promise of profit, a platform rate or a typical seller outcome.

Fulfil well and review actual results

Keep inventory updated, pack products for travel and respond promptly when an order needs attention. For food or other regulated goods, confirm the applicable licence, product standards, labelling and platform requirements before listing. Online discovery does not remove responsibility for safe products and truthful descriptions.

After a small pilot, compare actual settled proceeds, repeat orders, returns, time and customer questions with your existing channels. Continue only if the channel fits your products and operations. ONDC can offer another path to discovery; accurate listings and dependable fulfilment still matter after a buyer finds you.

Know who handles an order problem

For catalogue, order-alert or settlement issues, start with the Seller Network Participant you contracted with and retain the order ID and support ticket. A customer who placed an order starts through the buyer application used for the purchase. ONDC describes the next escalation route on its official complaints page. Keep these buyer and seller support routes clear in your own records.

Questions readers often ask

Does joining ONDC guarantee sales?

No. Discovery depends on current category and location coverage, buyer applications, listing quality, availability and customer demand. Begin with a pilot you can fulfil and review real outcomes.

Is there one standard ONDC commission?

The full commercial arrangement depends on your participant and other providers involved. Obtain current written charges for onboarding, selling, payments, logistics, returns and support rather than assuming a universal rate.

What should I check after my first genuine order?

Confirm the correct variant was delivered, record the actual fulfilment time, reconcile every statement deduction with the order and bank credit, and note any complaint or return. Do not create fake orders or reviews to simulate demand.
