How Indian Creators Can Earn on YouTube Beyond Ad Revenue

By FutureProof Blog · Published 26 October 2026 · 4 min read

A YouTube channel can earn in several ways, but none starts with a guaranteed payout. A creator needs videos viewers choose to watch, rights to use the material and a channel that meets the platform’s current rules.

This guide reflects YouTube’s official guidance checked on 26 October 2026. Requirements and feature availability can change, so check YouTube Studio and the linked policy pages before relying on a threshold.

Understand the eligibility levels

India is included in YouTube’s expanded Partner Program. Its current guidance lists an earlier access level for some fan-funding and Shopping features. It requires 500 subscribers, three public uploads in the past 90 days, and either 3,000 valid public watch hours in the previous 12 months or 3 million valid public Shorts views in the previous 90 days.

For Watch Page and Shorts Feed ad revenue, the listed threshold is 1,000 subscribers and either 4,000 valid public watch hours in the previous 12 months or 10 million valid public Shorts views in the previous 90 days. Channels also need to meet Partner Program policies and pass YouTube’s review. Features may have extra requirements.

The watch-hour route uses qualifying public long-form viewing. Shorts Feed watch time and viewing of private, unlisted or deleted videos do not count; paid-ad viewing is excluded as well. Account prerequisites include policy compliance, no active Community Guidelines strikes, 2-Step Verification, advanced-feature access and the required AdSense setup. See YouTube’s eligibility and qualifying-view guidance.

These numbers do not guarantee approval or income. See the current feature eligibility table and earning options.

Announced change from 1 February 2027

YouTube has announced higher ads and Premium entry thresholds for new creators from 1 February 2027: 1,000 subscribers plus either 8,000 qualifying watch hours in 365 days or 20 million qualifying Shorts views in 90 days. Existing YPP membership status is unaffected by that entry change. Relevant updated terms must be accepted by 31 January 2027 to continue earning from the affected features. Check the Earn tab and YouTube’s official change notice before planning around a threshold.

Learn the income routes

Depending on approval and location, creators may earn through:

  • Ads and YouTube Premium: revenue sharing on eligible views.
  • Memberships and viewer support: memberships, Super Chat, Super Stickers and Super Thanks where available.
  • Shopping: eligible product promotion.
  • Sponsorships: a brand pays for a clearly described integration or dedicated video.
  • Products or services: the creator sells something they own.
  • Affiliate links: the creator may receive a commission when a viewer buys through a tracked link.

Each route depends on viewer trust. Explain why a recommendation fits and follow platform and applicable disclosure rules for paid or affiliate relationships.

Make each video useful in its own way

Choose one audience and one question. A channel about everything is harder to plan than one that helps first-time home cooks, local sellers or people learning a particular skill. Decide what the viewer should understand or do after watching. Demonstrate the process, show an example, explain what can go wrong and link important sources.

Editing or AI tools do not automatically make a video original. YouTube says monetised content should be original and authentic; generic, repetitive or mass-produced videos may not qualify. Reused footage needs substantial original commentary or educational value, and creators need commercial rights to visual and audio material. Read the channel monetisation policies.

YouTube also requires disclosure of realistic, meaningfully altered or synthetic material in applicable cases. Assistance with an outline or script generally does not require that label. Disclosure itself does not remove monetisation eligibility; the disclosure guidance explains the distinction.

Ask whether viewers can tell what you personally tested, compared or explained. If not, add a real demonstration or context instead of making another interchangeable video.

Run a low-cost publishing test

Before buying equipment, plan four videos for one audience. Give each a clear question and useful example. Publish at a pace you can maintain, then review retention, comments and returning viewers in YouTube Analytics.

Track production time and expenses. If a video requires a paid tool, product or trip, include that cost when deciding whether the project is sustainable. YouTube’s feature guidance says views and watch time from paid ad campaigns do not count toward YPP eligibility thresholds.

A channel is a media project, not a quick-income button. Ad income varies, and sponsorships or product sales need an audience that trusts the creator. Start by publishing a useful, original series and use actual channel data to decide which income route fits.
