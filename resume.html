What is actually wrong with most resumes

Not talent. Formatting and framing. These are the five failures I find in almost every CV that arrives, and all five are fixable in an afternoon.

The software cannot read it

Two columns, a photo, a text box, a table, or your details in the header. An Applicant Tracking System returns a garbled mess and the file is scored as incomplete before a person opens it.

Duties instead of results

“Responsible for handling client accounts” tells a manager nothing. “Managed 40 accounts worth ₹2.3 crore, cut churn from 18% to 11%” tells them everything. Same job, different resume.

No numbers anywhere

If nothing in your CV can be counted, nothing in it can be believed. Almost every role has figures in it — team size, budget, volume, time saved. Most people simply never wrote them down.

The wrong words for the job

Your CV says “client servicing”, the posting says “account management”. The screen looks for the posting’s words. Matching them honestly is not gaming the system; it is speaking its language.

A summary that says nothing

“Hardworking professional seeking a challenging role.” Every rejected CV in the pile says this. Three specific lines about what you have actually done will outperform it every single time.

Two pages of the wrong things

Your 2014 internship gets a paragraph and your current role gets one line. Space follows recency and relevance, not chronology.

Correct. Send your CV, get the top third rewritten, decide. There is no card to enter, no trial to cancel and no follow-up if you go quiet. I would rather you judge the writing than a sales page.

What is an ATS, and does it matter?

An Applicant Tracking System helps employers collect and process applications. Some systems extract text from resumes, so complex columns, tables and graphics can make details harder to read. A simple layout can help, but no format guarantees an interview or a particular score.

Will you invent experience to make me look better?

No, and I will push back if you ask. Everything in the finished resume has to be something you can defend in an interview. A CV that wins an interview you then fail is worse than the one you have now, because it costs you the employer as well as the role.
